# Phase 4 - robustness

A scam filter is attacked by construction: the sender knows a filter exists, so
"does it work on clean text" is the wrong question. This phase perturbs the
validation messages the way a filter-evading sender would, measures how much each
model degrades, adds those perturbations to training, and re-measures.

Every number is regenerated by:

```bash
python -m scripts.evaluate_robustness --models tfidf-lr tfidf-lr-aug xlmr-base xlmr-base-aug
```

## 1. The six attacks

`scripts/adversarial.py`. Each is deterministic (seeded), changes the text only, and
leaves the gold label alone - so a perturbed row still has the right answer.

| Family | What the sender does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `char_swap` | leetspeak | `a->@  i->1  o->0  e->3  s->$` |
| `lookalike` | Cyrillic/Greek characters that render like Latin | `o->о  e->е  s->ѕ` |
| `transliteration` | romanised spelling drift | `click->clik`, `account->acount` |
| `spacing` | zero-width spaces, wide gaps, doubled spaces | `block` -> `blo ck` |
| `emoji` | emoji injected between words | `your account 💳` |
| `disguised_link` | link obfuscation | `bit[.]ly`, `b i t . l y`, `hxxps`, `ⓢ` |

Two details matter for honesty:

- `disguised_link` **never invents a link**. Appending a phishing URL to a genuine
  bank notification would change its label, not just its surface, so messages without
  a link are left alone (1,288 of 1,554 rows unchanged for that family).
- Attacks that cannot apply (leetspeak on Devanagari text) are **counted, not
  silently kept**: 137 rows are unchanged by `char_swap` because they are native
  script, and the harness reports that number.

## 2. Degradation before augmentation

F1 on clean text and under each attack, with the change from clean in brackets:

| Model | Clean | char_swap | lookalike | translit. | spacing | emoji | link |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tfidf-lr | 0.908 | 0.863 (-0.045) | 0.884 (-0.024) | 0.901 (-0.007) | 0.886 (-0.022) | 0.907 (-0.001) | 0.903 (-0.005) |
| indicbertv2-mlm | 0.993 | 0.910 (-0.083) | 0.961 (-0.032) | 0.993 (0.000) | 0.938 (-0.055) | 0.993 (0.000) | 0.994 (+0.001) |
| xlmr-base | 0.997 | 0.964 (-0.033) | 0.977 (-0.020) | 0.990 (-0.007) | 0.971 (-0.026) | 0.994 (-0.003) | 0.996 (-0.001) |

**Leetspeak is the attack that matters.** It is the worst family for every model, and
the worst single loss in the project is IndicBERTv2's -0.083: the encoder that wins
on clean text is the *most* fragile one, because its contextual confidence leans on
the exact surface tokens it saw in training. A model that is more accurate on clean
text is not automatically more robust to a sender who types `$` for `s`.

Emoji and transliteration drift cost almost nothing; the models read them as noise.
That is a mild reassurance, not a proof: 10 rows are unchanged by transliteration
because they contain no driftable word.

## 3. Augmentation, and what it recovered

`scripts/build_augmented_dataset.py` adds 0.5 perturbed copies per training row
(char_swap, lookalike, spacing, disguised_link), keeping every original row and label.
No validation row is used for augmentation, and the validation split stays clean, so
the improvement is not leakage.

| Model | Clean F1 | char_swap | spacing | mean flip rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tfidf-lr | 0.9082 | 0.8631 (-0.045) | 0.8860 (-0.022) | 0.017 |
| **tfidf-lr-aug** | **0.9198** | **0.9021 (-0.018)** | 0.9108 (-0.009) | **0.015** |
| xlmr-base | 0.9970 | 0.9639 (-0.033) | 0.9708 (-0.026) | 0.011 |
| **xlmr-base-aug** | **0.9980** | **0.9931 (-0.005)** | 0.9852 (-0.013) | **0.003** |

Augmentation did both jobs at once: it made the models *more robust* **and** slightly
*more accurate* on clean text (0.9082 -> 0.9198 for the baseline, 0.9970 -> 0.9980
for XLM-R). Leetspeak degradation for the baseline more than halved (-0.045 ->
-0.018); for XLM-R it fell by ~6x (-0.033 -> -0.005) and its mean flip rate dropped
from 1.1% to 0.3%. Three attacks went to exactly zero flip.

The accuracy gain is the interesting part and it is slightly counter-intuitive:
perturbing training text acts as a regulariser, so the model stops leaning on single
surface tokens and generalises slightly better even before any attack arrives.

**Cost: no extra latency.** The augmented baseline is a retrained model, not extra
machinery - 0.52 ms p50 on CPU against 0.51 ms un-augmented (`results/latency.json`).
The artefact grows from 10.2 MB to 16.6 MB, because perturbed training text adds
character n-grams to the vocabulary. That is the whole price, which is the argument
for baking robustness into training rather than bolting on a normalisation layer.

## 4. What this does not prove

1. **The attacks are the ones I thought of.** A determined sender mutates
   character by character with an adaptive loop against the model, not with six
   fixed templates. These are the cheap, static versions; an adaptive attack
   (EOT / gradient-guided token substitution) would be stronger and is not here.
2. **Adversarial training against six families does not generalise to a seventh.**
   The augmented model is robust to the families it saw; nothing guarantees it
   transfers to, say, script-mixing inside a single word.
3. **Still validation-only.** Every number in this phase is on a mostly-synthetic
   corpus. Robustness to a synthetic leetspeak attack is a weaker claim than
   robustness to a real scammer's real habits.
4. **Only two of the five model arms were re-trained with augmentation**
   (the baseline and XLM-R). IndicBERTv2, the prompted LLM and the LoRA model were
   not retrained, so no claim is made about their post-augmentation behaviour.

## 5. Recommendation carried into Phase 5

Ship **xlmr-base-aug**. On measured trade-offs it is the best model on clean text
(F1 0.998, CI 0.995-1.000), the most robust to every attack family (mean flip rate
0.003), and the only one whose leetspeak loss is under 0.01. Its open cost is size
and speed: 1.1 GB fp32 and ~32 ms p50 on CPU, against 10 MB and ~2 ms for the
baseline. Quantising the encoder is Phase 5's first job, because that is what turns
"1.1 GB" into a shippable number.
